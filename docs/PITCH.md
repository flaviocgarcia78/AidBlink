# AidBlink — Pitch script (2–3 minutes)

> 🌐 **Português (BR):** [PITCH.pt-BR.md](./PITCH.pt-BR.md)

> Colosseum asks for two distinct videos: a 2–3 minute pitch and a product demo
> of up to 3 minutes. This file is the **pitch**: problem, insight, product,
> market, why Solana. The demo is in `DEMO_SCRIPT.md`.

**Target length:** ~2:45 · **Tone:** calm, concrete, human.
**Speakers:** 1–2. Camera on the speaker for the pitch; no screen needed until
the last shot.

---

### 0:00–0:25 — The human problem

> "Every day, people need something small and urgent. One meal. One bus fare to
> get to a clinic. The help exists — there are donors, there are organizations —
> but it gets lost in the distance between the person who gives and the person
> who receives."

Show: a simple photo or a short scene of a bus stop / a community kitchen.

### 0:25–0:50 — Why this is hard

> "Organizations collect donations, but it is hard to show where each dollar
> went. And the people who need help the most are exactly the ones who cannot
> deal with a crypto wallet, a seed phrase or transaction fees. So most
> on-chain donation tools stop at the wallet — the donation arrives, the aid
> doesn't."

### 0:50–1:20 — The insight

> "We keep crypto exactly where it is good: funding, transparency and
> auditability. And we keep the human flow simple. AidBlink is emergency aid in
> a single link."

Reveal three words as you speak them: **Contribute · Administer · Deliver.**

> "A supporter opens a link — a Solana Blink — and contributes in one tap. The
> organization sees a transparent pool and approves a real need. The person
> receives a one-time voucher for a meal or a bus fare. No wallet required."

### 1:20–1:55 — The product

> "The supporter contribution is a real Solana transaction. A small Anchor
> program records each contribution as its own account on-chain and moves the
> SOL to the treasury in the same instruction — so the list of contributions is
> read from the chain, not from our database. The organization records only a
> pseudonymous reference — no personal data on the blockchain. When a need is
> approved, the system issues a signed voucher with a QR code. A partner scans
> it and the benefit is delivered — exactly once. A second scan is rejected.
> That single-use rule is what makes a voucher a voucher, not just a picture of
> a QR code."

### 1:55–2:25 — Why Solana and Blinks

> "Blinks are the perfect entry point: a shareable, wallet-native link means we
> never have to build a whole blockchain application for the donor. Solana gives
> us fast, cheap, verifiable transfers, and our on-chain program makes every
> contribution auditable by anyone — the transparency dashboard reads directly
> from it. It also lets us anchor each redemption for auditability. And on the
> receiving side, the person only ever sees a QR code."

### 2:25–2:45 — Ask / close

> "AidBlink does not confuse a donation on-chain with aid delivered. We close
> that gap — transparently for the donor, simply for the person helped. This is
> AidBlink: emergency aid in a single link."

Optional one-liner on market/impact if time allows:

> "The same flow works for food banks, community centers and mutual-aid groups —
> any organization that already delivers essentials, now with an auditable
> pipeline."

---

## Delivery notes

- **Do not** over-explain blockchain. The jury knows it. Spend the time on the
  human gap and on the one-time voucher insight.
- Emphasize the honest boundary: *contribution is real, benefit delivery is
  controlled by the organization.* This is a strength, not a limitation.
- Have the demo ready to cut to. Keep this video within 2:45 so it feels
  complete.
